# Content review notes

Reviewed 2026-09-11 against the sources below. Passages and question scenarios are newly written instructional adaptations; wording is not copied from the course. Each item carries source IDs that resolve to URLs and attribution in the bundled JSON. EMBL-EBI course material is licensed CC BY 4.0; its authors are Reza Salek, Stephen Beisken, and Laura Emery. | Concepts | Source and check |
| --- | --- |
| Molecules, metabolome, genetic/environmental context | [What is metabolomics?](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/metabolomics-introduction/what-is-metabolomics/) — small molecules, sampled biological systems, and genetic/environmental influences. |
| Examples of metabolites | [Small molecules](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/metabolomics-introduction/what-is-metabolomics/small-molecules/) — sugars, amino acids, and lipids. Numeric metabolite-count estimates were deliberately omitted. |
| Snapshots and pathways | [The metabolome and metabolic reactions](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/metabolomics-introduction/the-metabolome-and-metabolic-reactions/) — dynamic state, time points, enzyme-catalyzed pathways and product-to-substrate handoffs. Pool amount versus rate questions use a basic measurement distinction, not a claim that a static profile measures flux. |
| Study context and variation | [Design experiment](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/metabolomics-introduction/designing-a-metabolomics-study/design-experiment/) — representative sampling, external influences, and avoidable variability. |
| Diet, medication, collection timing, participant variation | [Barnes et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4964969/) — dietary and drug contributions, timing, and study-design considerations. Independent-person examples distinguish biological sampling from repeated measurement. |

The introductory scenarios draw logical limits from those concepts: observing a group difference does not isolate a cause; one tissue does not automatically represent another; a snapshot is not a time series; and repeated measurement does not create independent participants. These are teaching scenarios, not accounts of actual experiments.

Checks performed: source alignment for the central claims, one intended best answer per item, explanation consistency, two alternate questions per concept/level, ASCII passages, and prerequisites supplied by each compatible passage. Correct options are shuffled only for display; stable IDs are retained in history.

Remaining review: independent scientific review, distractor plausibility with real learners, and calibration of levels 3–4. Eighty items provide more headroom than the original forty-item minimum, but the bank is finite. Delayed reassessment is explicitly labeled and is not evidence of permanent mastery. Do not infer medical treatment decisions from educational examples.
